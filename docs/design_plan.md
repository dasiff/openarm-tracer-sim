### 3.4 Stance finder [DECIDED]

Fast queries, no brute-force floor search.

- Inputs: task points (the sampled skill path points), each labeled left / right / either (either prefers both), optional required approach direction; furniture map in two layers (base height and arm height), built from the scene geometry; the room walls get collision in the scene (they have none now). [DECIDED 2026-10-16] The VLA chooses the hand itself (prompts do not name the arm), so a skill's points are normally labeled "either"; "left" or "right" only when the arm is fixed (Phase A is left only). The output includes each arm's reach score for each point at each stance, because the demo generator picks the arm from it (§5).
- For each pedestal height and robot heading (every 5 deg): take each point's required map's horizontal cross-section at the point's height, flip it around the point, overlap all flipped outlines; subtract furniture enlarged by the robot footprint at that heading.
- Heading robustness: intersect with the regions at neighboring headings (+/-1 deg).
- Score each floor spot by distance to the nearest edge of the valid region (distance transform); real-hole islands count as edges. Scores are distances to the edge of the valid region; combine multi-point scores by minimum (for the arm rule of §5 each arm's score is rounded to 1 cm, so near-ties go to left); rank "either" points reachable by both arms higher: a stance where both arms can reach is preferred to one where only one can, because the VLA picks the hand and the stance must work for either.
- For each spot, record the valid pedestal height range; prefer spots valid across a range; choose a height mid-range.
- Pre-filter: drop stances whose path segments pass through furniture enlarged by an arm's thickness.
- Verification: check the top ~5 stances with inverse kinematics along the whole sampled path, checking all arm links against furniture and the other arm; keep the best that passes. (RAIL safety filter comes later on top.)
  - **The stance requirements and the ranking rule [DECIDED 2026-10-19, David; `src/task_config.STANCE_REQUIREMENTS`, `src/stance_checks.py`, `experiments/stance_from_map.py --select`]:** one set of requirements, the same in the finder's selection, the home pose search (`experiments/home_pose_search.py`) and `home_pose_test`. With both arms at home and the neck at its framing pose: (1) home pose clearance: every moving arm part (fingers and hand included) at least 40 mm from the bench, the hotplate and every other object and piece of furniture; (2) placed beaker: at least 40 mm from a beaker resting on the hotplate; (3) neck repeatability: the neck framing solve (`src/neck_framing.py`) moves no neck joint by more than 0.06 rad under 0.1 degree of arm noise (8 perturbed solves, the first four are the `neck_once_test`'s); (4) occlusion, by pixel area in the eagle camera's image (a segmentation render with and without the robot, `OcclusionMeter`): the robot hides none of the beaker's pixels and at most 20 % of the hotplate's; (5) framing slack: with the neck at the best pose it can take, the beaker's and hotplate's corners and both grippers (tool point, hand, finger tips) are at least 10 % of the image from every edge. All depend on the pedestal height. For each candidate position the heights of its valid range (1 cm steps) that pass all of them form runs of consecutive heights; the widest run is the candidate's passing range and its middle is its height (if the middle falls between two grid heights and fails, the nearest passing one). **Ranking rule:** among the candidates with a passing range, prefer the widest pedestal height range; then take the middle of that range; ties in width are broken by the arm coverage of the eagle image at that height (the smaller first; [DECIDED 2026-10-20, David]: the share of the eagle image's pixels the robot's arms cover, hands protruding into the image, measured with the same segmentation render as the occlusion; a preference, not a requirement, both grippers must still be in frame), then by the finder's tolerance there; the first whose IK verification passes is the result. In the home pose search the same preference enters the score: the key is the smallest margin up to 0.3, plus 2 % of the mean margin, minus 3 times the arm coverage (a fraction of the image), so once every margin is comfortable the search prefers the pose whose arms cover less of the image. (Until 2026-10-19 the candidates were ranked by tolerance alone, which chose a stance that passes at one height only.) Nothing is typed into the config but the result, labeled as finder output.

(Before this revision the sim oracle decided and a VLM checker was a later replacement.)
2. **Failure handling (revised 2026-10-21, David; replaces the paper's mark-failed-and-advance, E5).** The paper marks a failed step failed and goes on because its steps are independent; ours depend on each other (no place without a pick), so skipping is not the default. (a) A failed MANIPULATION step is retried up to `ORCH_CFG["retry_max"]` times (default 1). A retry: the failing arm alone returns to its zombie pose (the module's `recover()`; the other arm is not commanded, an object it holds stays held), the world model is refreshed, the step's objects are grounded again, the skill runs again, and the completion check fires again as usual. Reason: the VLA's skills start from the zombie pose (§6), so this is the retry the VLA can do. (b) After the retries the operator is asked through the existing operator interface (console and the viewer's status text: the step, the reason and the checker's image path; choices retry again, skip, abort; the choice is logged). (c) An unattended run (no operator present) stops the plan with the reason instead. (d) No retry, straight to (b) or (c): a navigation step, a grounding failure, a place whose manipulated object is not in the hand, a pick whose object is already in the hand, an object knocked over (its up axis more than `knocked_over_deg` from vertical), and a module error that is a clearance or contact abort (`error_kind()`); these read the world model and the module, never the oracle. (e) Every attempt is logged (`state["attempts"]`: attempt number, outcome, reason, the checker's verdict and the oracle's, the decision). Consequence: a failed completion check is retried only when the hand is empty afterwards (a missed grasp); a pick check that fails with the beaker in the hand, and a place check that fails with the beaker out of the hand, go to the operator. Answering the question from the viewer (keys) is still LATER (§10); a scripted operator answers in tests. Every failure is logged with its step and its reason (the failure log above).
3. **Scripted manipulation stand-in.** Until the VLA exists, the manipulation module is driven by the post-processing primitive tags of the plan (pick, place) plus the target object, which select the scripted skill. The step text is still passed through unchanged to the manipulation module's interface, which is what the future VLA will read (§7.3), so replacing the stand-in changes no other module.
4. **Timing.** Synchronous and in sim time: the physics waits while a module thinks (as in §7.3). The LLM is called once, before the episode starts, to make the plan.
5. **Swappable modules, each behind its own interface:** the planner (the LLM and its prompt), navigation (the scripted staging with the stance finder), manipulation (a command source in the control loop, `src/control_loop.py`), the completion checker, and the world model. The orchestrator knows only these interfaces.
6. **Plan format.** Exactly the paper's segments (Appendices C.1 and D), plus its post-processing tags: `{"task_name", "segments": [{"type": "navigation" | "manipulation", "description", "completion_criteria", "target"}]}`. `completion_criteria` is an end state, not an action; `target` is given for navigation segments only (null for manipulation). The post-processing tags of each manipulation segment are: (a) the primitive it maps to (pick or place), with that primitive's completion rule; (b) whether it is the last manipulation step before a navigation step, and only those steps get completion checks. Consecutive manipulation segments are grouped into one unit, whose completion criterion is the last step's. The primitive tag and the target object drive the scripted stand-in (item 3).
7. **Navigation targets.** A navigation step's target is resolved through the world model to a stance from the stance finder (§3.4), preferring stances where both arms reach (the VLA picks the hand; §3.4 ranks such stances first).
8. **LLM plans and tests.** LLM plans are cached by instruction, so tests and replays do not call the model and are deterministic. Each module is tested in isolation with fakes (a fake planner returning a stored plan, a fake checker, a fake world model); the whole pipeline is then tested with the fakes replaced one at a time.

### 14.1 The camera and the rule (fixed camera)

- **Camera model [STAND-IN, David 2026-10-22, M158]:** the Orbbec Gemini 335 (same family as the Gemini 305 wrist camera, §14.3), with depth; the specs are in 14.4. The camera David first named, the "Galexia Avant Pro", stays unidentified (M154: no datasheet found). **Depth is for navigation and perception only (§13); the VLA uses colour images only.** The sim's camera optics become the Gemini 335's colour sensor in step 2 part B (vertical field of view 55 degrees, 16:9, 640 x 360); the wrist cameras are unchanged.
- **Mount [FIXED, position and angle PLACEHOLDER until the hardware team decides, §11; the placeholder is chosen in step 2]:** the camera is fixed to the torso link `openarm_body_link0`, so it rides with the pedestal. There is no neck and no camera joint.
- **Face / head [David 2026-10-22, M159]:** the head moves to the top of the torso, right next to the camera; visual only (no mass, no collision), hidden from all policy cameras; the "faces the viewer" drawing trick stays.
- **Mount search [M161]:** the camera's tilt (pitch) and a few mount spots on top of the torso (a little forward or back, a little higher), facing straight forward, no roll, are chosen by a search at the TASK_CFG stance and pedestal command 0.155 with the arms at the zombie pose: both hands, the beaker and the hotplate inside the image with the 10 % margin, 0 % of the beaker and at most 20 % of the hotplate hidden, and the camera's collision box at least 5 mm from everything; the candidate with the largest smallest margin wins. The result is a PLACEHOLDER until the hardware team decides (`experiments/camera_mount_search.py`).
- **The view moves only with the pedestal height.** Nothing moves the camera while driving, parking or during a step; there is no drive pose, no look-ahead, no framing, no follow mode and no neck key. For bench work the pedestal stays at one height (`TASK_CFG["pedestal_q"]`), so the view is the same at every stance.
- **The rule: "inside the fixed view".** A target counts as in view when its image position keeps at least 10 % of the image width and height from every edge and nothing hides it (occlusion by the pixel-area measure, `OcclusionMeter`, from a segmentation render of this camera with and without the robot). Every camera check is this one question: the stance requirements, the null navigation step (F3, M155: every target of the next step within the arm's reach and inside the view), the stance selection and the stop of B6 ("outside the fixed view"). Nothing aims or optimises; no solver, no repeatability check.
- **Visibility requirements (the stance and home pose checks, §3.4):** with the camera at its fixed pose (and the pedestal at its height), the robot hides none (0 %) of the beaker's pixels and at most 20 % of the hotplate's, and the beaker, the hotplate and both grippers at home are inside the view with the 10 % margin. The neck spread requirement (0.06 rad) is dropped; the framing slack becomes the same "inside the fixed view" test. Among stances that pass, the one whose arms cover the least of the image is preferred (§3.4).
- **Superseded (M153):** the SO-101 neck and its four joints, the framing rule that aimed at the targets' bounding-box centre (M091), the least-movement rule and the null-step reframe (M135, M049, Stage 6 / M151), the repeatability / jiggle checks, the drive pose, the neck keys, and following the grippers.
