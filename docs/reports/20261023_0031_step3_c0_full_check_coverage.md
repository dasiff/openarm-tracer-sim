# Step 3, C0: what the full check covers today (read only, no code changed)

Written for: David, before part C (the bench stance template). Code: `src/full_check.py` (class `FullCheck`, `PathClearance`, `task_points`), `src/stance_checks.py` (`StanceChecks`, `OcclusionMeter`), `src/arm_collision.py` (`ArmCollisionChecker`), `src/eagle_view.py`. Settings: `FULLCHECK_CFG`, `STANCE_REQUIREMENTS` in `src/task_config.py`.

## The order of a check (`FullCheck.check`, full_check.py lines ~368 to 470)
1. At 3 pedestal heights (the middle, +2 cm, -2 cm): `StanceChecks.home_clearance` (both arms at the zombie pose against every scene geom, limit 40 mm) and `beaker_on_hotplate_clearance` (the arms at home against a beaker STANDING on the hotplate, limit 40 mm). Fails by name `home_clearance` or `placed_beaker`.
2. For the nominal stance and each error on its own (x +-1 cm, y +-1 cm, heading +-1 degree, pedestal +-2 cm: 9 stances, `_error_stances`): `PathClearance.body_distance` (the whole robot, both arms at home, camera box and battery included, against the world geoms; fails `body`), then `_solve_stance` (the IK path check: one joint solution per task point, `_admissible`: no arm collision with the robot, hand and fingers at least 1 cm and the other links clear of the bench, hotplate and objects, no elbow flip along a path; fails `ik`).
3. At the 3 heights: `StanceChecks.view_margin` (fails `view`) and `StanceChecks.occlusion` (fails `occlusion`).

## (a) Does a place step's check include the held object in the hand along the path? NO.
The IK path check solves only the 12 task points (`task_points`: 4 grasp-band heights at the beaker, the same 4 at the set-down over the hotplate, and the same 4 plus 10 cm "above"). They are pad-centre positions, and `_solve_stance` tests the arm, hand and fingers of the left arm at each solution (`_admissible`, `PathClearance.arm_distances`). The world geoms exclude the beaker (`PathClearance.world` leaves out everything under the "task_beaker" body, because the beaker is the target) and nothing is attached to the hand. So the beaker in the hand is not modelled at the set-down and above points: not its body against the hotplate, the bench, the camera box, the other arm or an unknown object on the table. Also not checked: the motion BETWEEN the points (the scripted skill's OVER, DOWN, IN, UP, BACK, HOME moves are not swept; only the waypoints, and the elbow-flip test between consecutive points of one path). The only held-beaker related check is static: arms at HOME against a beaker standing on the hotplate (`placed_beaker`). This is the gap that bit the 14 mm shifted stance in Stage 5 (the full check passed, the real place stopped 8.4 mm short at its first waypoint).

## (b) Does it check the fixed eagle camera's view of the targets? YES, at the zombie pose only.
Step 3 above: `view_margin` puts the robot at the stance with both arms at home and projects the view points (`view_points`: the beaker's and hotplate's corners and both grippers' tool point, hand and finger tips) into the fixed camera's image; the smallest margin must be at least 10 % (`STANCE_REQUIREMENTS["view_min_margin"]`). `occlusion` renders a segmentation image with and without the robot (and, in the full check, with unknown objects on the table counting as occluders, R2i): the beaker 0 % hidden, the hotplate at most 20 %. Both are done at each of the 3 pedestal heights. Not checked: the view while the arm works (the arm and the held beaker in the image, the hand hiding the target during the skill), and the beaker's view at the DESTINATION after it has been moved.

## (c) Does it check the base footprint and the creep-in path against furniture? The footprint at the stance, yes; the paths, no.
`PathClearance.body_distance` at each of the 9 error stances: the whole robot (arms at home) must not touch the bench, the hotplate, the room's other geoms or the objects (`body_clearance` 0). That is the final pose only. Not in the full check: the approach (the DRIVE route to the approach spot, which the navigation core plans and verifies at runtime with the real footprint, `src/nav_smooth.py`), the straight CREEP_IN from the approach spot (`staging_approach_back_m`, 0.2063 m) under the open bench, and any sweep of the footprint's heading change. The creep-in is protected only at runtime by the staging's clearance and lean watch (`staging_source.watch`).

## (d) Which targets the finder's chosen stance (TASK_CFG) was computed for
The scene's 12 points (`task_points`): the beaker at (1.00, 0.66) m and the hotplate at (0.80, 0.66) m (20 cm apart on the open bench, 14 cm from its front edge), the grasp band 45 to 60 % of the beaker's height (`BEAKER_CFG["grasp_band"]`, height 0.10 m), the left arm, heading -90 degrees, the zombie pose. The stance (0.765, 1.085) at pedestal 0.155 is the finder output of `experiments/stance_from_map.py --select` (the candidates ranked by the width of their passing pedestal range, the middle of the range chosen), checked by the requirements (home clearance, placed beaker, and, until M153, the old neck checks) and the IK verification (`experiments/stance_sensitivity.py`: all 12 points solve, the open fingers 15 mm from the bench and the hotplate, joint margin 30 degrees, xy tolerance at least 7 cm, heading +-8 degrees, pedestal +-4.2 cm). It serves BOTH objects at once: the targets' centre is (0.90, 0.66), 0.425 m ahead of the base and 0.135 m to its left.

## (e) How it decides which arm passes
It does not decide: `SIDE = "left"` (full_check.py line 46) fixes the arm. The reach-map ordering (`maps[("left", k)]`), the IK chain, the joint bounds, the collision filter (the right arm held at home) and the hand geoms (`HAND_BODIES`) are all the left arm's; the points' `"arm": "either"` field is not read. A stance that only the right arm could serve fails. The quick search (`src/stance_finder.py`) does handle arms: `_allowed_arms(points)` keeps the arms the points allow, `region_scores` scores each arm, and `arm_for(stance)` picks the better score (a near-tie goes to the left arm); the pipeline's manipulation module takes its arm as a parameter (`make_pipeline_task(arm="left")`).

## What this means for part C (my reading; for you to confirm or correct)
1. A template stance is checked by exactly these things; a shifted spot (+-5 cm, +-3 cm) that passes is not guaranteed to work for the place, because of (a). I suggest adding the held beaker (attached to the hand along the set-down and above points, and tested against the hotplate, bench, camera box and objects) before shifts are trusted, or a stricter error budget for the place.
2. `task_points` is built for this scene only (the beaker and the hotplate of TASK_CFG). "The next step's targets" for a pick or a place at other objects need a generic points builder from the grounded entries (pick: the grasp band at the object; place: the set-down and above at the destination). That is new code in C.
3. The template is an offset "from the object it serves", but this stance serves two objects. I would express it as an offset from the pick object's position (the beaker): (-0.235, +0.425) in the world for yaw -90 (0.765 - 1.00 = -0.235 in x, 1.085 - 0.66 = +0.425 in y), i.e. 0.425 m ahead and 0.235 m to the right of the beaker. For a place alone, the offset from the destination would be a second number. Tell me which you want: one offset from the first target of the run, or one per primitive.
4. Each null-step test is one full check (about 27 s warm, 40 to 90 s cold on this VM), and the full check needs its own simulation (about 2 GB): inside the pipeline process that is two simulations at once (peak about 4.4 GB; with the 4 GB swap it fits, but it is slow). Offline before the run (as in `stance_select_pipeline_test`) it costs nothing at run time but cannot see the world as it changes. Which do you want?
5. The full check's 12 points need the objects' positions at the time of the check; for the second step (the place at the hotplate) the beaker is in the hand, not on the bench: its pose is not where `task_points` assumes for the set-down heights.
