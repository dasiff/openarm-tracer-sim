**d. My reading (interpretation, not a further test).** Both: the **toe-in** is why the contacts are on the tip side (a pad pair that closes tips first touches a round or faceted beaker toward the tips: 1.9 mm for a round beaker), and the **facets** are why it is as far as 4.6 mm (a flat-on facet is touched at its edge) and why the two contacts are not opposite. The model's 24 sides (M081) are an approximation of a round glass beaker, so part of the "near the tips" is an artifact of the beaker model; the toe-in is a property of the finger mesh.

## Step 2: twisting friction at the finger pads (row M189)
**a. The change.** `src/beaker.py` gives the contact pairs of the two finger collision geoms with the beaker's wall and bottom segments `condim 4` (`BEAKER_CFG["pad_condim"]` = 4); the palm pairs and every other contact stay at `condim 3`. The torsional coefficient is `BEAKER_CFG["contact_friction"][1]` = **0.0012 m**, taken from physics, **not tuned to the tilt**: in MuJoCo the largest twisting torque is the coefficient times the normal force, so it has the unit of a length; the sliding friction 0.4 (M081) times the radius of a small contact patch of **3 mm** gives 0.4 x 0.003 = 0.0012 (a uniformly loaded circular patch would give two thirds of this, 0.0008; I took the round number on the upper side). The earlier 0.005 entry had never acted (condim 3 ignores it) and corresponds to a 12 mm patch radius, too large for "a few mm". The rolling entry stays.
