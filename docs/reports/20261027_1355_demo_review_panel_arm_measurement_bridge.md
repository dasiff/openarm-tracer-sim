## 4. Left arm motion, measurement only (item 4)
**What was measured.** The same moved-layout episode was run again and recorded (it is deterministic: 10728 ticks, 71.5 s, SUCCESS, the same numbers as the video's run). The recording was replayed with state verification (bit-identical) and every physics step (750 Hz) of the left arm's seven joints was saved: commanded position (the ctrl the replay reads), actual position and velocity. Acceleration and jerk are finite differences of the velocity at the control tick (150 Hz, used below) and at the physics step (750 Hz, noisier, in the files). Jerk is the largest absolute value over the seven joints, in rad/s^3. A boundary window is +-0.5 s around the first tick of a phase; "mid" is the middle half of a phase. Files (git-ignored): `data/arm_motion/baseline_20261027_132008/` (`left_arm.npz`, `metrics.md`, `metrics.json`, `plot_full.png`); plots with every phase boundary marked as dashed lines are copied to `docs/reports/figures/20261027_arm_motion/baseline_over.png` and `baseline_up.png` (sim time axis; the video time is sim time minus 1.4 s). Scripts: `experiments/left_arm_motion_measure.py`, `experiments/left_arm_motion_report.py`; `src/replay.py` got a read-only `step_hook`.

**(a) Peak jerk at each boundary versus mid-phase** (150 Hz, rad/s^3; commanded jump = the largest one-tick change of any commanded joint at the boundary tick, in milliradians, against the typical change per tick around it):

| PICK: DOWN -> PICK: IN | 53.33 | 94 | 8 | 39 | 2.03 | 0.42 | 0.004 / 0.018 |
| PICK: IN -> PICK: CLOSE | 56.54 | 9241 | 39 | 3 | 0.01 | 0.02 | 0.021 / 0.222 |

Reading it: the boundary OVER to DOWN (sim 49.42 s, video 0:48.0) is quiet (37, against 484 inside OVER and 8 inside DOWN). The jerk is inside OVER. The two big numbers at boundaries are the closing of the gripper (IN to CLOSE, 9241: the fingers hit the beaker; 296 000 at 750 Hz) and the start of PLACE DOWN (mid 1628, a contact phase, not investigated). The start of OVER itself (window 1125) is mixed with the end of the base's creep-in vibration (the arm shakes while the base creeps, jerk up to 3000 in CREEP_IN, the known arm swing); the window cannot separate them. After PICK UP starts (57.55 s) the mid-phase jerk is 49 with one speed bump at 58.82 s (peak 0.35 rad/s): mild, as you saw; nothing sharp, but the commanded target steps at its start (below).

**(b) Do the commanded targets jump at the boundaries?** The commanded POSITION does not jump (0 to 3.3 mrad in one tick, i.e. at most 0.5 rad/s), but the commanded VELOCITY does: at DOWN to IN, CLOSE to UP, OPEN to BACK, PLACE OVER to DOWN and BACK to HOME the commanded joint steps by 1.6 to 3.3 mrad in the first tick, against a typical 0.05 to 0.5 mrad (30 to 60 times the typical step, a step from rest to 0.25 to 0.5 rad/s in one 6.7 ms tick). The actual motion then follows smoothly (jerk at these boundaries 16 to 160). Inside OVER the commanded velocity has the same kind of steps (plot, 47.0 s and 47.9 to 48.2 s). So the targets are continuous but not smooth: the velocity is not.

**(c) Inside OVER: one smooth speed-up and slow-down, or pulses?** Pulses. The planned time of OVER is 1.0 s (`min_move_s`, from the log line "OVER (move) ... 1.0 s planned"), but it took 2.49 s (46.93 to 49.42 s). The solver (mink) limits every joint to 0.5 rad/s (`MINK_CFG["max_joint_speed"]`); the commanded speed sits at that limit on 52 % of the ticks of OVER (joints 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7), so the arm cannot follow the planned 1.0 s Cartesian path: the pad lags behind its reference and catches up once the reference has stopped at 47.93 s. The commanded joint speeds therefore plateau at the limit early (one smoothed commanded peak at 47.35 s) and the set of joints at the limit changes 14 times between 48.18 and 49.13 s (each change is a different solution of the solver: a step in the commanded velocity of those joints). The actual motion has three speed peaks, at 47.67, 48.07 and 48.31 s (0.4 and 0.24 s apart, 2 to 5 Hz), and its joint speeds reach 0.665 rad/s, above the 0.5 rad/s command limit: the position servos overshoot when the commanded velocity steps. Those are your uneven bursts (46.9 to 48.7 s). In PICK DOWN, IN and UP, and in PLACE OVER, DOWN, BACK the commanded speed never reaches the limit (0 to 3 % of the ticks) and the actual motion is one peak per phase (or two for PLACE phases); PLACE HOME is like OVER: limit on 39 % of the ticks, 3 actual peaks (67.4, 68.2, 68.5 s).

**(d) Is the bounce in the data the replay reads, or only in the video?** It is in the data. The replay reads the recorded ctrl (commanded targets, every physics step) and re-runs the physics; the video renders pictures of that replayed physics at 30 Hz. The commanded velocity in the ctrl already has the steps described in (b) and (c) (commanded peak jerk 14 000 to 240 000 rad/s^3 at 150 Hz at the boundaries and in OVER), and the actual motion measured in the same replay shows the three pulses. It is not a rendering artifact: the pulses are at 2 to 5 Hz, and the power of the joint velocities above the video's Nyquist rate (15 Hz) is 3 % in OVER, so sampling at 30 Hz does not create or hide them.

**(e) How the phase moves are generated today** (`src/mpvi_modules.py`, `src/mink_arm.py`, `src/rate_limit.py`):
- ONE move per phase (OVER, DOWN, IN, UP, BACK, HOME ...), not one continuous path. Each move is a straight line of the pad centre in Cartesian space from the MEASURED pad pose at the start of the phase to its waypoint, with the orientation turning by spherical interpolation; its time is `max(min_move_s 1.0 s, distance / speed, angle / turn_rate)` (speed 0.12 m/s in transit, 0.04 m/s for DOWN, IN, UP, BACK).
- The time law is minimum jerk (`min_jerk`: 10u^3 - 15u^4 + 6u^5), so the REFERENCE has zero speed and acceleration at both ends of every phase. Each tick (150 Hz) the reference goes to mink's differential inverse kinematics (frame task on the pad, weak posture task toward home, joint limits with a 10 degree margin, the 0.5 rad/s joint speed limit, collision avoidance); the result is integrated into the joint command `q_cmd`. A sag correction (integral gain 3 per second on the measured pad pose error, capped at 40 mm and 0.1 rad) is added to the reference. The HOME "joints" move at the end is a joint-space minimum-jerk move.
- The next phase starts when the previous one is settled (reached within 3 mm and 2 degrees AND all joints under 0.02 rad/s for 0.2 s). So the ACTUAL speed is near zero at each boundary (0.003 to 0.024 rad/s before it, as the table shows); it is not exactly zero. The commanded velocity is not zero for one tick at the boundaries listed in (b).
- Smoothing that exists now: the minimum-jerk time law on the Cartesian reference; mink's joint speed limit; the loop's "interpolate" mode (linear ramp of the ctrl over each 150 Hz tick, so no step within a tick). There is NO acceleration or jerk limit and NO filter on `q_cmd`; the 50 Hz bridge of Part A is not in the path unless turned on.
- Why the commanded velocity steps (my reading, not tested): the reference restarts from the measured pose, and the mink solution jumps by the difference between where the command is and where the arm is (sag), limited only by the 0.5 rad/s clip; and in OVER, the planned time is shorter than the joint speed limit allows.

## 5. Part A: the command bridge
**Built** (design plan section 1, your specs unchanged): `COMMAND_BRIDGE_CFG` in `src/task_config.py` (one block; every number with a plain-word comment, its unit and row M193): `enabled` False, `rate_hz` 50, `max_joint_velocity_rad_s` 0.5, `max_target_step_rad` 0.25, `max_tracking_error_rad` 0.25. The loop setting is `cfg["command_bridge"]` (None, or a dict overriding entries of the block; `{"enabled": True}` turns it on) in `src/control_loop.py`. It sits between the sources and the arm targets, on the 14 arm joints only: every 3rd tick it (1) suspends while any arm joint's command is more than 0.25 rad from the measured joint (the command stays put until the arm catches up), (2) rejects a target as a whole if it is more than 0.25 rad from the current command on any joint (the previous accepted target stays), (3) moves the command at most 0.01 rad toward the target and applies it; the "interpolate" mode ramps over the 3 ticks. A target outside a joint's limits is rejected as a whole, checked before `validate_command` clips it. Every rejection and suspension (and its end) is logged with the joint, the value and the limit and the episode phase (`bridge_log` in the loop's result; the first 20 printed; a repeat in a row is merged and counted). The bridge is off during a replay (the replay reads the recorded ctrl). Test `experiments/command_bridge_test.py`, 14/14 (OFF applies a 0.6 rad step at once; ON limits a 0.2 rad step to 0.01 rad per update at 50 Hz and reaches it; a 0.6 rad step and a target outside the limits are rejected and logged with joint, value and limit; with the tracking limit set to 0.003 rad the suspension and the resumption are logged and the target is still reached; the gripper and the pedestal are not limited); added to `run_tests.sh`. Two of my own choices, in M193: when the arm part of a command is rejected its gripper part still goes through; the real bridge's "joint state older than 0.1 s" suspension is not built (the sim's state is never stale). `experiments/demo_video.py` now runs with the bridge ON by default (`--no-bridge` turns it off); nothing else turns it on.
