# Reach heat maps of the bench top (filtered maps, pedestal 0.16 m)

Written for: David (the question was: visualize the reach maps; what the visualizations show).

## What was made

- `reach_heatmap_combined.png`: for each bench-top point (5 cm grid), the number of parking spots that can reach it from any approach bin, either arm, any heading.
- `reach_heatmap_bin_00.png` to `reach_heatmap_bin_11.png`: the same count, using only that approach bin. Bin k is centred at k x 30 deg from the robot's forward axis (torso +x, turning left). All 12 bins share the combined map's colour scale.
- `reach_heatmap_failures.png` and `reach_heatmap_failures_stats.json`: the 100-layout sweep on the combined map (part c).
- `reach_heatmap_counts.npz`: the counts (grids `total` (ny, nx) and `per_bin` (12, ny, nx), bench-point centres `xs`, `ys`).
- `reach_heatmap_make.py`: the script (`--geometry`, `--render`, `--sweep`).
- `reach_heatmap_geometry.json`: the robot and bench values the projection used (copy of `data/reach_heatmap/geometry.json`).

## Method and definitions

- Maps: **filtered**, `data/reach_map_before_bench` (the bench is in the maps). Unfiltered (`data/reach_map`) was not used here.
- Projection: the same transform as stance selection. Shoulder world xy = base xy + the torso-frame shoulder offsets turned by the heading (`stance_select.shoulder_xy`). Height = `stance_finder._torso_z(q) + shoulder z`. Offsets and heights come from `stance_finder.robot_geometry` on the loaded model (`data/reach_heatmap/geometry.json`):
  - shoulder offsets in torso frame: (0, +0.031, 0.698) left, (0, -0.031, 0.698) right
  - torso height above base origin at q = 0: 0.3048 m; lift factor 1.0
  - base height above floor: 0.1527 m
  - shoulder height above the floor at pedestal 0.16 m: 0.1527 + 0.3048 + 0.16 + 0.698 = 1.3155 m
- Pedestal: 0.16 m (David's choice for the projection).
- Reach point height: bench top surface 0.96 m + `PICK_PLACE_CFG["grasp_height_frac"]` (0.525) x `BEAKER_CFG["height"]` (0.10 m) = 1.0125 m. This is my choice (the pad centre on a beaker standing on the bench); it is a config value, so it can change.
- Parking spot: a floor cell (5 cm grid, base origin) outside the bench-top footprint. A bench point counts a spot if some heading (5 deg steps, all 72) and either arm reaches the point from one of the 12 bins' maps. Per-bin counts use only that bin's map (still any heading).
- Floor cells under the bench top are excluded. No other furniture, home-clearance or footprint filter is applied.
- Bench grid: 88 x 32 points (5 cm). Max count 411 spots; 441 of 2816 points have zero.

## What the visualizations show

Combined map (`reach_heatmap_combined.png`):

- The count is hot along the rim of the bench top and cold in the middle. The brightest points are the four corners (about 400 spots); the long edges at y = +/-0.8 m are about 230 to 260; the short ends are about 300 to 350.
- The cold part is one central block, roughly |x| < 1.6 m and |y| < 0.25 m, where the count is zero. It is not at the edges or corners. It sits in the centre of the bench.
- The map is symmetric in both x and y.

Per-bin layers:

- Every bin has the same basic shape (a rim with a cold centre). The bins differ only in strength: the mean count across the bench, as a share of the combined mean (125.7), is 0.98 for bins 2, 4, 8 and 10; 0.86 for bins 1, 5, 7 and 11; 0.70 for bin 0; and 0.66 for bin 6 (approach from behind the robot, 180 deg).
- So the back bins (4 to 8) reach almost as many points as the forward ones. This matches the earlier note that the back bins reach as many cubes as the forward ones. These per-bin layers do not show a clear direction effect, because each bin's count takes the union over all 72 headings (see the caveat below).

Geometric pattern (step 2, description only):

- Shape of the reachable zone: a rounded band or frame around the bench, hot on the rim and cold in the core. The core is a rectangle close to the bench's centre.
- Arm reach envelope: the core is cold because the bench is about 1.6 m deep. A point on the centreline is about 0.8 m from the front or back edge, and the arm reaches only about 0.7 m from the shoulder, so no floor cell outside the bench can reach it. Points near the edges are within reach from the floor just outside. The ends (x = +/-2.2 m) are reached from the sides, which gives the corners their high counts.
- So the cold zone is the centre, not the edges or corners. The cold zone is one connected block, not scattered.

Sweep overlay (part c):

- All 100 layouts failed (0 of 100). Each failure's stop message was classified as:
  - "repositioning": stop mentions navigation, transit or a second run. **0 layouts.**
  - "no_cover": no stance covers the objects. **69 layouts.**
  - "full_check": stances were found, but the full check rejected every candidate. **31 layouts.**
- The classification is my definition from the stop messages (David did not define it). It is in `classify()` in the script.
- Median count at the beaker point: 56 for no_cover layouts, 196 for full_check layouts. Median at the hotplate point: 98 for both. The overall median over the usable grid is 98.
- In the figure, the no_cover beakers sit mostly in the central cold block. The full_check beakers sit near the bench ends and the front and back rims.

## Caveats (please read)

1. **Map set mismatch in part c.** The sweep's selection ran on the unfiltered maps (`data/reach_map`; its docstring says so). The heat map is from the filtered maps. The overlay therefore compares failures from one map set against counts from the other. This needs a decision: rerun the sweep on the filtered maps, or make the overlay from the unfiltered counts. The rerun takes about 75 minutes of MuJoCo time, so I did not start it.
2. **Shoulder height formula.** The message asked for shoulder height = base height + pedestal height (0.3127 m). That disagrees with the stance transform (1.3155 m, above). With 0.3127 m, every bench point would be 0.70 m above the shoulder, outside the maps' height range (-0.51 to +0.51 m), so the whole map would be zero. I used the stance transform, since you asked for the same transform as stance selection. Please confirm.
3. **Heading union hides the direction.** Each bin's count takes all 72 headings. A bin then means "this approach is possible at some heading", so the per-bin layers mostly show how much each approach contributes, not from which side of the bench. A direction-specific version would keep only headings that face the bench (the stance finder's `heading_window_deg`, 45 deg). Not done; tell me if you want it.
4. **Filtered maps have a stamp that matches the unfiltered one.** `reach_map_before_bench` was restamped on 2026-10-22 (see its build summary). The stamp does not tell the two sets apart; the cube counts do (169,941 vs 177,488 for left bin 0).
5. **The MuJoCo load.** One MuJoCo process (`--geometry`) ran to read the robot and bench values through the stance code's own `robot_geometry`. It ran after the sweep finished. It is not needed for the rendering, which reads the saved maps and `geometry.json` only.
