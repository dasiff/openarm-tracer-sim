## Step 1: Part 3, servo formula (no run)

The loop's arm actuators are MuJoCo position servos in the scene model (`load()`; `combined_robot.xml` uses torque motors but the scene's arms are position actuators). Gains from the scene model (`actuator_gainprm`, `actuator_biasprm`, `dof_damping`, `dof_frictionloss`):

- Position servo: force = kp (ctrl − q) − kv q̇ − d q̇ − f·sign(q̇) + (gravity, handled by the ctrl offset `tau_ff / kp` in `src/gravity_ff.py`).
- Steady tracking lag at constant joint speed q̇: e = (kv + d) q̇ / kp, so the lag time is τ_j = (kv_j + d_j) / kp_j.
- The friction term adds a constant offset f / kp in the direction of motion.

| 1 | 70 | 2.75 | 0.40 | 45.0 ms | 0.0014 rad (0.08°) |
| 2 | 70 | 2.50 | 0.40 | 41.4 ms | 0.0014 rad (0.08°) |
| 3 | 70 | 2.00 | 0.40 | 34.3 ms | 0.0014 rad (0.08°) |
| 4 | 60 | 2.00 | 0.40 | 40.0 ms | 0.0017 rad (0.10°) |
| 5 | 10 | 0.70 | 0.40 | 110.0 ms | 0.0100 rad (0.57°) |
| 6 | 10 | 0.60 | 0.40 | 100.0 ms | 0.0100 rad (0.57°) |
| 7 | 10 | 0.50 | 0.40 | 90.0 ms | 0.0100 rad (0.57°) |

### Reading the sweep

1. **The solver part is large in two situations, and they are different.** (a) The per-joint cap binds: sideways 200 mm/s is 91 to 256 ms of solver lag in every pose (cap 1.00 on every tick); down 100 mm/s at home, mid, near and new1 and new2 has cap shares of 0.7 to 1.0 and a solver part of 19 to 194 ms. (b) The margin pins a joint: at home and near, upward 25 mm/s has a solver part of 140 to 145 ms with the margin active on every tick (joint 2 held at its margin bound). The new poses are away from the margin, and their upward solver part is 0.5 to 1.3 ms. So the upward lag at home and near is a position effect (the home pose sits at the margin), not a speed effect.
2. **The servo part is direction-dependent, and it is not the same across poses.** At 25 mm/s with the layer bypassed, forward is +68 to +88 ms in all five poses, and sideways is −104 to −138 ms in all five poses. Upward and downward change with the pose: upward at 25 mm/s is +84 ms at home but +1 ms at new1; downward at 25 mm/s is −44 ms at mid and −102 ms at near.
3. **Sideways is a fixed distance, not a time lag.** Bypassed, the sideways gap is about −2.3 to −2.8 mm at every speed tested: 25, 50 and 100 mm/s at home, mid and near, and 25 and 100 mm/s at new2 (new2 has no 50 mm/s cell). New1 is −3.5, −3.1 mm at 25 and 100 mm/s. A lag that scales with speed would give a constant time (about 110 ms at 25 mm/s and 28 ms at 100 mm/s), not a constant distance. The forward gap is the opposite: about 2.0, 3.2 and 5.5 mm at 25, 50 and 100 mm/s, which fits a time lag of about 46 ms plus a small offset. The constant sideways offset could be a friction-type offset in the joints; the Part 3 friction offsets are 0.001 rad on joints 1 to 4 and 0.010 rad on joints 5 to 7, and multiplied by a lever arm of about 0.1 to 0.5 m they give roughly 0.1 to 5 mm. This is not checked.
4. **The solver changes the servo part, not only the solver part.** With the solver on, the sideways 25 mm/s servo part is −138 ms bypassed but −16 ms at new1 and +42 ms at new2. The solver changes the mix of joint speeds the arm is asked to follow, and the joint servo lags differ about 2.5 times between the shoulder and the wrist (Part 3), so the pad-space servo part changes sign with the mix. Not separately verified.
5. **Pose dependence of the total gap.** Translation lag changes with pose most where the margin is active (home, near, and mid for downward) and where the solver cap binds. In the new poses, which have no margin contact, the solver adds little except at the cap.

## Answers to the five questions (plain language)

1. **Is the hand-speed ceiling the solver's cap?** For sideways, yes. At 200 mm/s the cap binds on every tick in all five poses, the solver part is 91 to 256 ms, and the bypassed arm follows the same ramp with about −10 ms. For downward at 100 mm/s the cap binds on 70 to 100 percent of ticks and the solver part is large. The servo itself is not the ceiling for these cells.
2. **How does the gap split between the solver and the servo?** It depends on the cell. Where the cap binds or the margin pins a joint, the solver part is most of the gap (sideways 200, down 100 at home/mid/near/new2, upward 25 at home and near). Where neither applies, the servo part is almost the whole gap (forward at all speeds, sideways at 25 to 100 in the new poses, downward at 25 to 50). The servo part on its own (bypass) is about −110 to +88 ms at 25 mm/s and changes sign with direction and pose.
3. **Does the measured servo lag match the Part 3 formula?** For forward at 50 to 100 mm/s, the bypassed lag is about 46 ms per unit speed plus about 1 mm, close to the 40 to 45 ms formula for joints 1 to 4. For sideways the lag is not a time (it is a constant distance of about 2.7 mm), so the formula's time constants do not describe it. The probe fits (Step 2) matched the formula within about 10 percent on the sideways bypassed run.
4. **Does the gap depend on pose?** Yes. The upward and downward servo parts change strongly with pose (upward 25 mm/s: +84 ms at home, +1 ms at new1). Forward and sideways at 25 mm/s are close across poses (forward +68 to +88 ms; sideways −104 to −138 ms).
5. **What does it mean for the correction?** A single time-lag per speed will not describe this: the sideways gap is a distance, the forward gap is a time, the upward and downward gaps change sign with pose, and the solver's cap and margin add large terms at high speed and at some positions. The data point to a model with a per-direction term, a speed ceiling from the solver, and a pose-dependent term for the vertical axis. I have not chosen a model.
