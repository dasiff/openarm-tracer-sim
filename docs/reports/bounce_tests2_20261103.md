Labels: **shown by the data** (read from a saved file or log line), **calculated from the data** (a formula applied to saved values, stated), **guess** (a possible explanation not tested here), **unknown** (not determined).

Terms: **trim** is the sag correction, an integral (running sum) of the hand's pose error that nudges the path to cancel droop. Its parts are **x, y** (sideways and forward), **z** (vertical) and rotation. **e** is the hand's height minus the planned height, in mm. **Lag** is the first peak of e after DOWN starts (the hand is above plan because it has not kept up with the planned descent). **Undershoot** is the first trough of e (the hand is below plan after overshooting). **Waypoint** is the planned point the DOWN move ends at. **Tick** is one physics-control step (150 per second).

## 5. B against A: the z-only freeze

The x part ended more negative than in A (−2.16 mm against A's −0.94 mm), so the z freeze also changed x (**calculated: the x part ended 1.2 mm further from zero than in A**; its cause is **unknown**).
- **Undershoot (shown by the data):** −4.40 → −1.28 mm, a fall of 3.12 mm (71 %).
- **Lag (shown by the data):** +4.99 → +6.31 mm, a rise of 1.32 mm. The lag grew when the vertical trim was held. Calculated from the same data: in A, the vertical trim falls by about 4 mm during the lag, which pushes the reference down and reduces the hand's error; holding it removes that push. This is consistent with the z part reducing the lag, but it is a **calculated** reading of the trim's sign, not a direct measurement of cause.
- **Swing (shown by the data):** 9.39 → 7.59 mm (3 s and whole-step windows agree).
- **Step:** **completed** (final distance 0.20 mm). This is the difference from last round: with x, y and z all frozen the step failed at 3.45 mm; with only z frozen it completes. The sideways (x) correction is therefore what brings the hand to the waypoint.

**Which half of the swing does this test explain?**
- **Undershoot: the test removed most of it (shown by the data).** The undershoot shrank 71 % with the vertical trim held. **Guess:** the vertical trim is the main cause of the undershoot. The remaining −1.28 mm is not explained by this test, and whether x or y contribute to it is **unknown**.
- **Lag: the test made it worse (shown by the data).** The vertical trim reduces the lag, so the lag is partly the vertical trim's doing. **Calculated from the trim signs:** the vertical trim pushes against the lag. The 4.99 mm lag is still mostly present with the trim held (+6.31), so most of the lag comes from something other than the vertical trim. **Guess:** the rest is the DOWN motion itself (see section 6).

**Which half of the swing does this test explain?**
- **Lag: explained by speed (shown and calculated).** It halves when the descent is halved and scales roughly with speed (0.56 against 0.50). **Guess:** the lag is the hand's tracking delay behind the planned descent, which grows with the descent speed.
- **Undershoot: reduced more than the lag (shown).** **Guess:** the undershoot is the vertical trim's overshoot, which is driven by the size of the lag it has to correct. This would explain why it fell further than the lag, but the link is not measured here.

**Calculated from the config and the log, not a new measurement:** the planner's timeout for a waypoint move is `timeout_factor` (3.0) × the planned time + `timeout_extra_s` (6.0) (`src/mpvi_modules.py` line ~427 and `src/task_config.py` line 443).
