**First failing step: HOME of the place.** Cause, by class:
- **Joint limits:** not the cause. All joints are inside their hardware ranges at the end (joint 6 at 12.0 deg, range ±45; joint 2 at 0.0, range −190 to 10; joint 4 at 126.1, range 0 to 140). The home pose itself is inside them.
- **Tracking limit:** not the cause. The joint tracking error is 0.57 deg at the end; no tracking suspension occurred in this run (no "[bridge]" event).
- **Contact:** not the cause. The elbow record shows no contact of the elbow housing, upper arm or forearm with the bench (touched False, 0 N). The hand has 0 N of contact force during HOME.
- **Reachability:** not the cause. The arm's pad at the end of HOME is the pad at home_q_left: forward kinematics of the logged HOME-end joints and of home_q_left agree to 0.1 mm (0.3035, 0.1515, 1.4747 m and 0.3035, 0.1516, 1.4747 m, robot frame).
- **Class: a controller/command conflict in the joints step (most likely the sag layer, M262/M264).** The HOME-joints step sets the command to home_q over 1 s, yet the applied joint 6 command stops at 11.4 deg from 93.8 s on, and the other joints stop where they match the pad pose (joint 1 25.6 vs 22.6, joint 2 0.0 vs −14.7, joint 3 7.0 vs 15.5, joint 5 3.7 vs −3.7 deg). The pad, however, is at the home pad. So the arm holds the hand pose, not the home joints (the 7-joint arm has a null-space choice). The sag layer's own docstring (src/sag_layer.py line 23) names this case: "the trim also runs on joint-only moves (recovery's HOME)". Not confirmed: the trim values are not logged.

## Step 7: why the guard clamped (read only)
Method: scratch/guard_clamp_analysis.py (poses from the logs; the guard's own elbow_z function and the model's forward kinematics, both for both arms) and a logging-only copy of the guard-on pipeline (scratchpad/guardon, ElbowGuard.clamp wrapped to record its inputs; the guard itself unchanged; data/ in the copy: out/guard_calls.json).

- Guard's table height: not used. The guard's code compares only the minimum ELBOW_GUARD_CFG["min_joint_z_m"] = 1.1048 m (derived from the table height 0.960 m in the config comment). table_top_z_m is documentation.
- Pedestal the guard used: the row's commanded pedestal, 0.160 m (the measured pedestal was 0.1594 m; the difference moves the elbow by 0.6 mm). The stance pedestal 0.12 m is not the value used at this pick: the staging pedestal in the run is 0.16 m.
- Elbow heights at the clamp (guard-on probe, logged measured joints, base at its settled height 0.1527 m):

| 67.40 | right | 1.1230 m | 1.1230 m | 0.000 mm | 1.1048 | no |

- The guard-on logging run's first clamp was at 67.16 s: the guard saw a target elbow of 1.1048 m and a measured elbow of 1.1055 m. The logged commanded targets (what the source asked for) of the left arm were at 1.1008 to 1.1022 m at 67.4–68.7 s, below the minimum, so the guard held the applied joints at the boundary (the clamp output is at 1.1046–1.1048 m).
- Correction to my earlier IN-move report: the elbow height I calculated there (1.17 m, "unexplained") came from my own error: I set the base height from load_task's start state (the XML height 0.22 m) and not the settled height 0.1527 m. With the settled height the guard and the model agree exactly, as above.
- **Would a rate-limited step (the target moves no faster than the bridge allows) still reach the grasp height?** No. At the grasp pose the guard-off run reached (pad z 1.0128 m, DOWN end at 71.37 s), the left elbow is at **1.0985 m**, 6.3 mm below the minimum (the guard's own elbow_z and FK agree). The lowest left elbow in the guard-off run over DOWN is 1.0988 m (t = 71.0 s). A rate limit changes only how fast the path is followed; the end pose is the same, so the grasp height needs the elbow below the minimum for this arm configuration. An IK solution with a different elbow configuration was not tested (no new IK run).
